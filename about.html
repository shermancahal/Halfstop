Halfstop
About

It started out of pure selfishness

I wanted all of it in one place, on a map that actually shows the ground.

The routine this replaces

If you shoot on the road you know it. One app for sunrise and sunset. Another for the moon. A website for the Milky Way. A different one for aurora. Weather somewhere else entirely. And then a notes app, or a screenshot folder, or a pile of dropped pins in Google Maps where you sort of keep track of where you have been and where you still want to get to.

And we know how unreliable some services can be once they get acquired by larger companies.

I have been an avid user of Gaia GPS, onX, Google Maps, The Photographer’s Ephemeris, PhotoPills and PlanIt Pro. They all have their strengths and their weaknesses. Halfstop is not an attempt to beat any of them at what they are good at — it is the one map I wanted that did the whole job in a single place.

What you get for a pin

Drop a pin anywhere and you get sunrise, sunset, moonrise, moonset, golden hour and blue hour for that exact spot. Eclipse tracks and aurora forecasts. When the Milky Way core is up, how high it climbs, and whether the sky is going to be dark and clear enough for it to matter. Cloud cover, fog, snow and storm tracks over the ground you are actually driving toward.

And the ground underneath it

I pulled in every free, reliable data source I could get my hands on. USGS topographic quads and aerials. Hillshade and contours. State level maps. Public land overlays so you know whose ground you are standing on. Drone ceilings and advisories.

On pricing, because I would rather just tell you

A lot of it is free. Some of it genuinely costs me money every time someone uses it, so that part is not. Place search costs me per lookup and is free anyway; saving the view as a picture costs me nothing, so it is free too. Syncing, weather, offline downloads, routing, photographs on a waypoint, the state detail maps and the extra basemaps are the paid side. The full split is on the homepage, and it is the same list the app itself enforces.

There is a free month if you want to try the paid side. You start it yourself from the plan in settings after signing in, it needs no card, and when it runs out it just stops. Nothing to cancel, nothing to forget about. One per account.

Where it is right now

The web version is live and works well, including on a phone. Sign in and it syncs between browsers. You can start using it today.

I am waiting on refreshed Apple Developer credentials, and once those land it goes into TestFlight for real testing. I would like it out before peak fall foliage. An Android port comes after that.

Tell me what is missing

I built this for how I shoot, which means I have almost certainly overlooked how you shoot. Tear into it. Write to support@halfstop.app and it reaches me directly.
