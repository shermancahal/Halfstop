Planning a Shoot

Where the light times are

Select a pin, or press anywhere on the map, and the Details panel opens on the light. The bar across the top is the whole day — night, twilight, golden hour, daylight, and back down the other side — with now marked on it. Under the bar are the two numbers most evenings turn on: how long the evening golden hour lasts, and when blue hour starts and ends.

All of it is worked out for the coordinates of that pin rather than the nearest city, and it is arithmetic rather than a lookup. There is no request behind it, so it answers the same on a ridge with no bars as it does at the kitchen table.

Moon, Milky Way, aurora, eclipse and fog

Six tabs under the bar. Moon carries rise and set, the phase, and the percentage lit — the number that decides whether a landscape is lit or a sky is dark. Milky Way is the galactic core: whether it is up tonight, how high it gets, the window while the sky is properly dark, and the best nights over the coming weeks.

Aurora is a chance for your latitude, read from NOAA rather than modelled here, with the Kp index beside it. Eclipse is the next lunar eclipse, its contact times, and whether the moon will be above your horizon for them. Fog is a likelihood hour by hour, with the ingredient that decided each hour named next to it.

Which way to face

The last tab draws the plan on the ground: bearings from the pin to the sun, to the moon and to the galactic core, with the Milky Way band itself as the wide curve. The slider moves time, and the lines swing with it.

That is the point of drawing them. A table can tell you the sun comes up on a bearing of 71°; only the map tells you the ridge is in the way.

Planning a dark night

Light pollution and sky brightness as a Bortle class are both in the Conditions group. Turn them on to pick the ground before you plan the night, then fade them back so you can see the roads under them.

Why there are no solar eclipses

A lunar eclipse is one event the whole night side of the planet sees at the same moment, so it can be answered with arithmetic on your device. Where a solar eclipse falls is a different and far fussier calculation, and a wrong answer would send somebody hundreds of miles. It is absent rather than approximated.

Offline Access

Offline download

Saves the area you are currently looking at as a named region. It records the bounding box, a zoom range and which basemap was showing.

Map regions

The regions you have marked. Each one shows how much ground it covers, how many tiles that works out to at the zoom range you chose, and roughly how much space they will take.

Download for offline

Fetches the tiles and keeps them on the device. It stores the basemap you have selected plus any layers switched on over it, and the panel names what it is about to store before you press it.

What can be downloaded

Byways Topo, USGS Topo and Imagery, the state aerial and lidar layers, BLM and the FAA ceilings can all be downloaded.

Save as a picture

A photograph of the map as it looks right now, saved to your device. It is not the same as a downloaded region: a picture cannot be panned or zoomed, but it needs no account, no network and no space to speak of. On a phone in a canyon it is often the whole answer.

Sharing

Sending someone the view

The share button in the bar copies a link to exactly what you are looking at: the maps you have open, the basemap under them and the view itself. Opening it puts the other person on the same screen.

Sending someone a place

A saved waypoint or a dropped pin has its own Share, and the link opens the map over that coordinate with the name on screen.

It does not write to their folders. A link that quietly added a pin to somebody’s saved places would be a surprise; saving it is their decision, and the button for it is already there when they make it.

Sending someone a folder

By address, when you are both signed in. Put theirs in the folder’s Share with someone box and an invitation goes out; the folder is waiting in their list when they sign in with that address.

The grant is the address rather than a link, deliberately. A link is forwardable, and this is your collection of places: one “look at this” into a group chat and it is public. An invitation names one address, so a forwarded one is useless to anybody but the person it names.

Without accounts it still goes as a file. The GPX the folder already exports is offered to the share sheet on a phone and saved to your device otherwise, and anything that reads GPX will open it.

Working on a folder together

An invitation says one of two things: to view, or to edit together. Someone invited to edit can add, change and remove waypoints in that folder, and both of you keep your work. Waypoints are merged one at a time rather than the newer save taking the whole folder, so you adding a pin while they rename a different one leaves both. If you both change the same waypoint, the later change is the one that stands.

The folder itself stays yours. Somebody you invited cannot delete it, cannot refile it into their own folders, and cannot pass it on to anybody else. Withdraw the invitation whenever you like and it leaves their device on their next sync.

A trip is a folder with dates on it, so all of this is true of trips too, and the dates travel between your own devices along with everything else.

Layers

Basemaps and overlays

One basemap at a time — it is the ground everything else is drawn on. Overlays stack over it in any number, each with its own switch and its own opacity, so public land, forest roads, weather and airspace can be turned up or faded back without disturbing the map underneath.

Not responding

A red badge means that layer’s service is not answering. Some are known to be intermittent.

The drone layers

Nothing here is a flight authorisation. It is the FAA's own data drawn on a different map, and it can be out of date.

Drone ceilings

The UAS Facility Map: the grid LAANC reads when it approves a flight. The number is how high LAANC will grant that authorisation instantly — not a speed limit for the sky. Every cell on the grid is inside controlled airspace, and a Part 107 flight there needs authorisation whatever the cell says. A ceiling of 0 does not mean the airspace is closed; it means nothing is pre-approved and the request goes to further coordination or FAA DroneZone instead.

Outside controlled airspace there is no grid at all. That is Class G, where no authorisation is needed — an absent cell is an answer, not a gap.

Restrictions and advisories

Red is do-not-fly; amber is permitted with permission or with care. These are standing and scheduled restrictions only. Same-day temporary flight restrictions — wildfires, VIP movements — are NOTAMs, and they are not in this layer. The card links to the FAA's current list; check it before you fly.

Military operating areas are deliberately left out. They do not restrict civilian flight and are generally based well above where a drone flies, so drawing them would fill the screen with warnings that do not apply.

Directions and Trip Planning

What the trip planner does

It works out how far apart your stops are, how many days that is, and where the days break. It will also draw the actual road route.

Why there are no turn-by-turn directions

Turn-by-turn is Apple’s and Google’s ground, fought with their traffic data and their voice guidance, and this app is not trying to take it. The drive is planned and drawn here and then handed over: a stop goes to Apple Maps, Google Maps or Waze, whichever you already trust.

Account

Do I need one?

No. Everything you save stays in your browser and needs no account. Sign in and your folders sync between devices; sign out and they are cleared from that device, because they are on your account and come back the moment you sign in again. If the last sync did not go through they stay put instead, and the panel says so.

What syncing carries

Folders and the pins in them. Photographs stay on the device they were added to — only the identifier travels, so a picture taken on your phone will not appear on your laptop. Uploading megabytes per pin is not something to do to somebody without asking.

Exports

GPX by default, which is what Garmin, Gaia, AllTrails and every handheld will import. GeoJSON is offered alongside it for anything that wants the full properties.
